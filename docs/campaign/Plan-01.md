## 1. Campaign overview

- **Objective:** Turn the supplied speaker list into relevant, permission-based conversations about NEXOPS ONE's self-hosted DORA register tooling, its route to market, and the right people to advise or introduce.
- **Primary measurable goal:** Secure **4–6 short meetings or explicit follow-up conversations** with the 10 named contacts before or during the event.
- **Secondary goals:** Obtain 2–3 actionable introductions to compliance practitioners, potential design partners, or ecosystem contacts; record at least 5 specific product, adoption, or go-to-market insights; agree one concrete next step with each high-fit contact.
- **Campaign is not:** A generic mass pitch, a claim that speakers endorse the product, a request for investment from every investor, or a request for legal/regulatory sign-off from people whose listed remit does not establish that expertise.

## 2. Product and positioning context

NEXOPS ONE is presenting a self-hosted, open-core DORA register and readiness tool for Luxembourg fund managers and smaller regulated entities. The current event strategy positions the compliance engine as the initial product and the free Register Health Check as a low-friction way to start a customer conversation.

**One-sentence introduction:**  
“We’re building a self-hosted, open-core tool to help Luxembourg fund managers work with their DORA register. At Venture Days I’m looking for informed feedback on adoption, validation and routes to the right fund and compliance practitioners.”

**Proof to show:** a short, rehearsed offline walkthrough of what the current build actually does; a one-page product summary; and the Health Check booking page/QR code where relevant.

**Claim guardrails**

- Do not say the product is CSSF-approved, certified, or guaranteed to pass a filing.
- The event strategy says the official EBA taxonomy and validation rules are not yet loaded and the Profile A export is indicative. Do not present the product as an official filing validator or production-ready submission tool.
- Describe the Health Check only by its current, deliverable scope; it is a readiness aid, not legal advice, certification, or an assurance of regulatory acceptance.
- Do not imply any listed person or their organisation is a customer, partner, adviser, or endorser unless that relationship is separately agreed.
- Do not share confidential customer data or ask contacts to send registers at the event. Follow the site's stated NDA and secure-link process for any later Health Check.
- Treat fundraising as a separate conversation. Do not assume any speaker is considering investment or seeking a product pitch.

## 4. Core messages and asks

### Shared message

“The tool runs in the customer’s own environment and has an open-source core. We are validating the product and its route to market with Luxembourg financial-services practitioners; I’d value your perspective on [contact-specific question].”

### Ask ladder

1. **Primary:** 10–15 minutes for a focused perspective on one question.
2. **If time is short:** one specific answer or pointer to the right role/person.
3. **Follow-up:** permission to email a one-page overview or book a separate product walkthrough.
4. **Only when relevant:** ask whether a practitioner or organisation might be open to a later discovery conversation. Never request a register or confidential information in an event conversation.

### Channel variations

- **LinkedIn/email before the event:** personal, specific, low-pressure invitation; mention their panel only when listed in this plan.
- **At the event:** one-sentence context, one question, one small ask. Let the contact choose whether to continue.
- **After the event:** thank them, reflect one specific takeaway, and send only the material or next step they agreed to receive.

## 7. Assets and preparation checklist

### Must-have

- **One-page product summary:** problem, target user, current product state, deployment model, what exists now, limitations, and one contact route.
- **Contact-specific outreach notes:** name, role, panel time (if supplied), one reason for contacting, one question, one low-friction ask, source of contact details, and response status.
- **Offline demo:** current, tested build with a short 2–3 minute path and a PDF/screenshot fallback. Demonstrate only features confirmed to work.
- **Meeting QR/link:** use the current product or Health Check page only if the CTA matches the contact’s purpose. Investor/adviser contacts should not be presented with a customer booking ask by default.
- **Founder introduction:** 15-second and 45-second versions; practice answers on product maturity, validation rules, security/data handling, customer need, and commercial model.
- **Follow-up tracker:** contact, tier, outreach date/channel, reply, meeting time, notes, permission to follow up, agreed next action, due date.

### Nice-to-have

- A one-slide architecture/hosting diagram.
- A short product walkthrough video or screenshots in case the venue setup prevents a live demo.
- A separate Health Check handout for fund-manager prospects; keep it distinct from the investor/adviser product summary.

## 8. Success metrics and reporting

Targets are campaign hypotheses for this small, named-contact list, not prior performance benchmarks.

- **Primary KPI:** 4–6 confirmed 10–15-minute meetings or explicit follow-up conversations with the 10 named contacts.
- **Conversation KPI:** At least 8 substantive event conversations overall, including other relevant practitioners met through introductions.
- **Actionable learning:** At least 5 documented, specific insights on buyer pain, product validation expectations, procurement, or channel fit.
- **Introduction KPI:** 2–3 permission-based introductions to relevant compliance, fund-management, or financial-services technology practitioners.
- **Follow-up KPI:** 100% of agreed follow-ups sent by 17 October; each has a specific next step or a clear close.
- **Tracking:** Maintain the contact tracker and count only confirmed meetings and completed conversations—not connection requests, impressions, or vague expressions of interest.
- **Cadence:** Update daily during the event; review outcomes and next actions by 23 October.

## 10. Risks and mitigations

1. **Schedule or access mismatch.** The event dates in the supplied brief are 14–16 October, while the existing GTM document refers to 14–15 October. Confirm the organiser's current programme, venue, and meeting platform before sending calendar-specific requests.
2. **Role mismatch or over-personalisation.** A person's title or panel does not prove product validation, procurement authority, or investment interest. Use the contact map as a question hypothesis; confirm relevance in conversation and ask for referrals only with permission.
3. **Overclaiming product readiness.** The existing GTM strategy explicitly notes missing official EBA taxonomy/validation-rule work and an indicative export. Keep all event claims aligned with that state; do not promise CSSF acceptance, certification, or legal validation.
4. **Too many asks in one conversation.** Focus each conversation on one question and one next step. Offer a demo only if requested or useful to answer the question.
5. **Sensitive information exchanged informally.** Do not accept registers, customer data, or confidential documents at the event. Any later Health Check must follow the documented NDA and secure-transfer process.
